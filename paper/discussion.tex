\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence jointly supports.}
Four independent probes converge on the same reading of open-weight LLM physics
performance. (i) Accuracy on SciBench physics tops out at 25.7\% for the largest
model we ran and gains only $+4.6$ percentage points from a two-fold parameter
increase, while chain-of-thought prompting roughly doubles accuracy
(2.2--2.4$\times$ for the Qwen family) --- prompting matters more than scale.
(ii) The same model reaches 66.6\% on multiple-choice MMLU physics but only
21.1\% when it must produce the number itself, which rules out the "no physics
knowledge" explanation: the knowledge is present and retrievable, the numerical
execution is not. (iii) A perturbation that leaves physics and data bit-identical
and only rewrites surface phrasing flips 24\%--34\% of final answers in every
model we tested, without changing accuracy on average, i.e.\ the flips are
symmetric error jitter rather than a difficulty effect. (iv) Restoring clean
residual states recovers behaviour mainly from the early-middle layers
(peak at layer 8 of 28, plateau over layers 4--16) and collapses after layer 18.
Taken together: outputs are produced by computation, but the computation is not
a stable physical solver --- it is surface-conditioned pattern matching with a
fragile arithmetic tail.

\paragraph{Why the error taxonomy is itself uncertain (and why that is a
contribution).}
Our cross-annotator agreement ($\kappa = 0.133$, 60.2\% raw agreement over 495
samples) is low, and this is not merely a nuisance for our own error analysis ---
it reveals a fundamental limitation of the error taxonomy itself: the boundary
between ``misapplying a physical law'' (E1) and ``algebraic miscalculation''
(E2) is inherently ambiguous, even for strong LLMs. When a model applies the
wrong law, the resulting arithmetic is also wrong, so the observable output is
consistent with either label; conversely, an arithmetic slip inside a correct
derivation is textually indistinguishable from a mis-selected relation. We
therefore report only the coarse, annotator-robust statement --- E2 dominates and
E3 is rare under \emph{both} judges --- and deliberately do not quote precise
category percentages. This suggests that evaluating AI reasoning purely through
coarse-grained error categories is insufficient: taxonomy-based error analysis
inherits an annotator-dependent ceiling that is invisible unless agreement is
measured. We release our dual-annotated sample (495 wrong answers from two
models, each labelled independently by a same-family judge and a cross-family
judge) as a benchmark resource not only for physics reasoning, but for future
work on the robustness of LLM-as-a-Judge error attribution, where the
\emph{disagreement} between judges is the signal rather than noise.

\paragraph{Implications for mitigation.}
Two of our findings are directly actionable. First, since the dominant failure
mode is arithmetic rather than physical (E2), the highest-leverage interventions
are execution-side --- tool use / code interpreters, or explicit symbolic
bookkeeping --- rather than more physical pre-training data. Second, since 24\%--
34\% of answers move under a semantics-preserving paraphrase, single-prompt
evaluation understates the variance of a model's competence; reporting
paraphrase-consistency alongside accuracy is a cheap and informative robustness
metric. Our counterfactual probe tests whether the instability is recoverable at
inference time by adding structural scaffolding to the prompt (requiring the
knowns and unknowns to be written down explicitly), i.e.\ whether the deficiency
is a missing scaffold rather than a missing capability.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
All numbers come from a single GPU class (A800-80G) and a transformers backend
without continuous batching, so wall-clock times are not comparable to vLLM
setups, although the accuracy numbers are backend-independent given greedy
decoding. Causal tracing is limited to one open-weight model and to the subset of
problems that satisfy the strict inclusion rule (14 of 150 attempted), so layer
localisation should be read as a robust qualitative pattern (early-middle
concentration) rather than a precise per-layer estimate. OlympiadBench results
refer to the 43.3\% of problems with numerically gradable references and must not
be compared numerically with SciBench. Finally, all models are evaluated
zero-shot with a single fixed prompt per condition; prompt sensitivity is itself
one of the phenomena we measure, so no attempt is made to tune prompts per model.
