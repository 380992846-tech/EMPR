\title{\textbf{What a Single Accuracy Number Hides:\\
Separating Value-Level Wandering from Execution-Level Failure\\
in Physics Reasoning}}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- Abstract
\begin{abstract}
\emph{A single accuracy number cannot tell a model that computes from a model that
recognises the problem family} --- and, we show, it also cannot tell a stable model
from one whose answers wander. We audit what the headline metric misses with five
probes plus two controls on physics reasoning: a value-level perturbation
(rescale the numbers), a correctness-level perturbation (rephrase the question
while leaving physics and data bit-identical), a multiple-choice format control,
corrupt-and-restore causal tracing, and an error-type annotation checked by a
second, cross-family judge. Across \nbSciBench{} SciBench physics problems and five
open-weight models, three results do the work. First, on the measurement side,
paraphrase changes \pctChangedSevenBWording\% of final \emph{values} yet moves only
\nbWordingFlips{} of \nbWordingCovered{} items across the correct/incorrect
boundary, mostly in the \emph{favourable} direction (\nbWrongToRight{} items are
rescued by the paraphrase versus \nbRightToWrong{} broken; exact McNemar
$p=\pWordingExact$): retrospective re-scoring of the same
generations converts what a value-level comparison reports as a large difficulty
effect into a non-significant one with no systematic penalty. Second, on the
mechanism side, output execution and semantic correctness run on different
pathways: parseability is essentially layer-invariant while correctness is
depth-dependent, and on problems the model cannot solve, restoring any single
layer never exceeds \pctAccCleanWrongBest\% (Wilson 95\% CI
$[\wilsonCwLo,\wilsonCwHi]$). Third, recoverable behaviour occupies a
\emph{contiguous early-middle band} (layers \plateauLo--\plateauHi{} of
\nbLayers), and we show that single-layer claims are \emph{not identifiable} at
our sample size --- a negative identifiability result we treat as a finding, not
an apology. Two validity bounds accompany these: strict-format evaluation and
fallback evaluation give different ceilings on the same generations
(\pctNoAnswerCot\%--\pctNoAnswerStruct\% of outputs never emit the requested answer
marker), and two LLM judges agree at only $\kappa=\kappaAnnotators$ on an
error taxonomy whose E1/E2 boundary is not textually observable. Everything runs
on one 80\,GB GPU in about \gpuHoursTotal{} GPU-hours, and the dual-annotated
sample is released.
\end{abstract}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1 Intro
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Chain-of-thought prompting \citep{wei2022cot} is routinely credited with giving
language models ``reasoning'' ability, and physics benchmarks such as SciBench
\citep{wang2023scibench} and OlympiadBench \citep{he2024olympiadbench} are used to
demonstrate progress. The evidence offered is almost always the same shape: a
single accuracy number, compared across models or prompts. That number silently
aggregates decisions about what counts as the answer, which generations count as
valid, and which differences are real. This paper takes those decisions apart.

\paragraph{Thesis: the metric is the object of study.}
This is not a ``which model is better at physics'' paper. It is an audit of what
the headline metric --- accuracy on a benchmark, compared across prompts, formats
and models --- can and cannot support. Three things a single accuracy number
cannot distinguish, and that we measure separately:
(i) an answer \emph{value} that drifts under a semantics-preserving paraphrase from
an answer whose \emph{correctness} changes; (ii) producing \emph{a} number from
producing \emph{the right} number; and (iii) a behaviour that is
\emph{localisable} to a layer range from one that would licence a claim about a
specific layer. In each case we find that the metric as usually reported hides a
distinction that changes the interpretation, and in two of the three the honest
conclusion is a \emph{validity bound} on the evaluation rather than a statement
about the model.

Our probes are deliberately cheap and falsifiable. A perturbation that changes the
physics (rescale the numbers) should move a computing model; a perturbation that
changes only the phrasing should not. Causal tracing should localise whatever
computation exists. An error taxonomy should agree with itself across annotators.
Where a probe returns a negative or uninformative answer, we report the bound it
imposes rather than the conclusion we would have liked.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Baseline behaviour.} (\S\ref{sec:main}) Five open-weight models on
        \nbSciBench{} SciBench physics problems, with bootstrap intervals and the
        prompt/scale interaction made explicit.
  \item \textbf{Value vs.\ correctness.} (\S\ref{sec:sensitivity}) A paraphrase
        that leaves physics and data bit-identical, re-scored against the
        reference rather than compared answer-to-answer.
  \item \textbf{Execution vs.\ semantics.} (\S\ref{sec:causal}) Corrupt-and-restore
        tracing, with two metrics recorded per layer: is the emitted answer
        parseable, and is it correct?
  \item \textbf{Knowledge vs.\ format.} (\S\ref{sec:cross}) A multiple-choice
        control that holds the physics fixed and changes only the output format.
  \item \textbf{Validity bounds.} Error attribution across two judges
        (\S\ref{sec:errors}), prompt scaffolding (\S\ref{sec:scaffold}), and an
        output-format ceiling that caps what strict grading can see
        (\S\ref{sec:format}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A measurement claim, not a model claim} (strongest result): on the
        same generations, a semantics-preserving paraphrase changes
        \pctChangedSevenBWording\% of answer \emph{values} but moves only
        \nbWordingFlips{} of \nbWordingCovered{} items across the
        correct/incorrect boundary, mostly in the favourable direction and not
        significant under an exact McNemar test. Any evaluation that compares
        prompt variants by a single accuracy pass --- or by answer agreement ---
        can therefore read value-level wandering as a difficulty change. We show
        the two readings diverge, and that the direction of the error is
        predictable: agreement-based comparisons overstate the effect.
  \item \textbf{An identifiability result about layer localisation}: recoverable
        behaviour forms a contiguous early-middle band (layers
        \plateauLo--\plateauHi{} of \nbLayers), and single-layer claims are
        \emph{not identifiable} at our sample size --- the point-estimate maximum
        is statistically indistinguishable from several other layers inside the
        band. We report the band, the formal definition that produces it, and the
        identifiability limit as a result rather than as a caveat.
  \item \textbf{A representation-vs-output decomposition}: parseability is
        essentially layer-invariant (\pctParseCorrupt\% even under corruption),
        while correctness is depth-dependent; and on problems the model cannot
        solve, restoration never rescues them at any single layer
        ($\leq$\pctAccCleanWrongBest\%). Execution and semantics are separable,
        and the failures are capability limits rather than misplaced
        representations.
  \item \textbf{Two validity bounds on the measurement apparatus}, reported
        alongside (not instead of) the findings: between \pctNoAnswerCot\% and
        \pctNoAnswerStruct\% of outputs never emit the requested answer marker, so
        strict-format and fallback grading give different ceilings on identical
        generations; and two LLM judges agree at only
        $\kappa=\kappaAnnotators$ on an error taxonomy whose E1/E2 boundary is not
        textually observable. We release the dual-annotated sample as a resource
        for LLM-as-a-judge reliability research.
\end{enumerate}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2 Related
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Physics and science reasoning benchmarks.}
SciBench \citep{wang2023scibench} collects college-level textbook problems with
free-response answers; we use its physics subsets and restrict to the
\nbSciBench{} problems whose reference answers are numerically gradable.
OlympiadBench \citep{he2024olympiadbench} contains competition problems; only
\pctOlympiadScorable\% of its physics items have numerically gradable references,
a coverage limit we carry through every table. MMLU \citep{hendrycks2021mmlu}
provides multiple-choice knowledge questions and serves here as a format control
rather than as a target.

\paragraph{Causal tracing and localisation.}
Activation- and state-level interventions are standard for localising behaviour
in open models \citep{meng2022locating,stolfo2023mechanistic}. We use the simplest
variant --- corrupt the input, then restore the clean residual stream at a single
layer --- because it requires no training and yields a per-layer curve. In
factual-recall studies the recoverable signal typically sits in the middle of the
network --- for a \nbLayers-layer model, roughly the central third (layers
$\sim$9--19); our plateau sits \emph{earlier} than that, which we discuss in
\S\ref{sec:causal}.

\paragraph{Sensitivity and robustness of LLM answers.}
Prompt sensitivity is well documented \citep{sclar2023quantifying}, usually as a
nuisance to be averaged out. We instead treat sensitivity as the measurement, and
--- unlike most prior work --- we separate \emph{value} changes from
\emph{correctness} changes, which turn out to have very different magnitudes.

\paragraph{LLM-as-a-judge and error taxonomies.}
Using a strong model to label errors is common practice
\citep{zheng2023judging}, and its reliability is usually reported as agreement
with human labels. We contribute a case where two \emph{LLM} judges disagree
substantially on a subjective taxonomy, and argue that the disagreement is itself
the informative quantity.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 3 Method
\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Benchmarks and grading}
All correctness decisions are made by symbolic/numeric comparison. The final
answer is extracted by a precedence-ordered procedure (\texttt{ANSWER:} line,
taking the last occurrence; then \texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{\}}; then
``answer is $X$''; then the last number on the last line), normalised for Unicode
minus signs, superscripts and thousands separators, and compared to the reference
under a 5\% relative tolerance (0.01 absolute for near-zero targets).

Reference answers are parsed with a \emph{stricter} parser than model outputs: a
reference that is not a single number is marked unscorable and removed from the
denominator rather than counted as a model error, because a permissive parser
reads expressions such as $x^2+2x$ as $2$ and silently corrupts the denominator.
For SciBench physics all \nbSciBench{} references are numerically gradable, so the
denominator is the full set; for OlympiadBench only \nbOlympiadScorable{} of
\nbOlympiadAll{} are. Grading is covered by \nbSelfCheck{} self-test cases and
\nbTests{} unit tests, and an oracle check (feed the reference as the model
output) yields 100\% self-consistency. Full prompt templates are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.

\subsection{Models and inference}
We evaluate five open-weight instruct models: Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B-Instruct
\citep{qwen2024}, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct \citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, and
DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B \citep{guo2025deepseekr1}. Decoding is greedy with a
fixed seed, so every reported number is deterministic. We use the tokenizer's chat
template and left padding for batched generation. All runs use the
\texttt{transformers} backend: vLLM was \emph{not installed} in our execution
environment, so the code path that prefers vLLM never activated and no result
mixes backends. Under greedy decoding accuracy is backend-independent; only
wall-clock differs (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Perturbation probes}
Each probe is a deterministic transformation of the problem text:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{numeric}: scale physical values (physics intact, at most two
        numbers changed, exponents and power bases protected);
  \item \textbf{wording}: paraphrase the framing only, leaving physics and data
        bit-identical. This is the only variant whose reference answer remains
        valid, so it is the only one for which we report a paired correctness
        comparison;
  \item \textbf{swap}: exchange two distinct physical quantities, producing a
        physically inconsistent problem.
\end{itemize}
Because the correct answer to a \emph{numeric} or \emph{swap} variant is not
directly computable, those variants are scored by the distribution of output
change, and we report median and p90 rather than the mean (the sensitivity score
$|\Delta|/(|base|+\epsilon)$ is dominated by outliers when the base answer is near
zero). The \emph{swap} probe covers only 36 of \nbSciBench{} problems, so its
percentages are reported for completeness and carry no quantitative weight.

\subsection{Causal tracing}
The numeric quantities of a question are replaced by a semantically empty
placeholder; the corrupted prompt is run; then the clean residual stream is
\emph{restored} at exactly one layer at a time, all other layers remaining
corrupted. Restoration is applied during prefill only and never to decode steps,
and clean and corrupted sequences are aligned from the right because they
tokenise to different lengths. Only problems whose clean run was correct
\emph{and} whose corrupted run lost score enter the recovery rate, which we report
together with the skip counts.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 4 Results
\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The order of the subsections follows the audit: each starts from a number that is
normally reported and then reports what that number conceals.
\S\ref{sec:main} establishes the baseline and shows the prompt$\times$scale
interaction the accuracy matrix hides; \S\ref{sec:sensitivity} separates value drift
from correctness change; \S\ref{sec:causal} separates output execution from semantic
correctness and states an identifiability limit; \S\ref{sec:cross} holds knowledge
fixed while varying format; \S\ref{sec:errors} and \S\ref{sec:format} are the two
validity bounds; \S\ref{sec:scaffold} closes the loop by testing a mitigation. Read
in order, the sections support one claim: the standard single-number summary is
under-specified for the distinctions this benchmark is used to draw.

\subsection{How large is the prompting effect relative to scale?}
\label{sec:main}
Table~\ref{tab:main} reports SciBench physics accuracy for all five models under
both prompting conditions, with bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals.

\input{table1_main.tex}

In this setting the prompting effect is large relative to a doubling of parameters,
although the two are measured on different scales (a ratio versus a difference in
points), so the comparison should not be read as a magnitude ranking: for the
Qwen2.5 family chain-of-thought multiplies accuracy by \ratioThreeB$\times$,
\ratioSevenB$\times$ and \ratioFourteenB$\times$ at 3B, 7B and 14B, while going
from 7B to 14B adds \gapFourteenVsSevenCot{} points
(\accCotSevenB\% $\rightarrow$ \accCotFourteenB\%). The prompting benefit is
family-dependent rather than a law of nature: for Llama-3.1-8B the same comparison
gives only \ratioLlamaEightB$\times$ (\accCotLlamaEightB\% vs
\accDirLlamaEightB\%), and family can outweigh size --- the 3B Qwen model
(\accCotThreeB\%) outperforms the 8B Llama model (\accCotLlamaEightB\%). The
reasoning-distilled model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, \accCotROneSevenB\%) does
not beat its same-size instruct sibling, which we attribute to friction between
long reasoning traces and this benchmark's terse numeric answer format; we did not
run it answer-only, because its instruction-tuned behaviour emits reasoning traces
regardless of the prompt and a \emph{direct} comparison would not be
like-for-like.

\paragraph{What the accuracy number hides here.}
The matrix in Table~\ref{tab:main} reports accuracies; the interaction that matters
is in its last two columns, which no single cell expresses. Chain-of-thought helps
the Qwen family by \ratioThreeB--\ratioFourteenB$\times$ but Llama-3.1-8B by only
\ratioLlamaEightB$\times$, so ``prompting helps'' is family-conditional, and a
paragraph comparing two models at one prompt condition can reach the opposite
conclusion depending on which condition is chosen. Likewise the family-over-scale
inversion (Qwen 3B at \accCotThreeB\% versus Llama 8B at \accCotLlamaEightB\%) is
invisible if a study samples one model per family and reports a single score.
Neither point is new in kind, but both are exactly what a headline number discards.

\subsection{What the accuracy number hides, I: value drift vs.\ correctness change}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} reports the two perturbation probes that carry
quantitative weight for four models (a third, \emph{swap}, covers too few items to
support any claim and is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:swap}).

\input{table2_sensitivity.tex}

Changing the numbers moves the answer most of the time
(\pctChangedSevenBNumeric\% for Qwen2.5-7B), so the stated values do participate
in the computation: the model is not a lookup table. A paraphrase that leaves
physics and data bit-identical changes \pctChangedSevenBWording\% of answers, and
the pattern repeats across models (\pctChangedThreeBWording\%,
\pctChangedFourteenBWording\%, \pctChangedLlamaEightBWording\%);
Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity} visualises the contrast.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=.72\linewidth]{sensitivity_comparison.png}
  \caption{Share of final answers that change when physical values are rescaled
           (\emph{numeric}, \nbNumericCovered{} items) versus when only the phrasing
           is rewritten (\emph{wording}, \nbWordingCovered{} items), out of
           \nbSciBench{}; bars show Wilson 95\% intervals. The paraphrase bar is
           non-zero for every model --- but \S\ref{sec:sensitivity} shows this is a
           value-level effect, not a correctness-level one.}
  \label{fig:sensitivity}
\end{figure}

Crucially, ``changing the answer'' is not the same as ``changing correctness''.
Table~\ref{tab:wording-2x2} scores the paraphrase variants against the reference and
reports the paired 2$\times$2 counts. For Qwen2.5-7B the number of \emph{discordant}
items --- those crossing the correct/incorrect boundary, which is by construction
\nbRightToWrong{} $+$ \nbWrongToRight{} --- is \nbWordingFlips{} out of
\nbWordingCovered{}: \nbRightToWrong{} went correct $\rightarrow$ wrong and
\nbWrongToRight{} went wrong $\to$ correct (exact McNemar $p=\pWordingExact$). The
pattern is the same in kind for the other three models, though the direction of the
few discordant items varies. We therefore state the weaker and better-supported
claim: we detect no systematic \emph{penalty} from paraphrase --- if anything the
discordant items lean favourable --- and the effect is not significant in either
direction. The paraphrase instability is real, but it is
\emph{value-level wandering} rather than a systematic loss of competence --- a
distinction that a single accuracy number hides entirely.

One caveat on that characterisation: we did not stratify the discordant items by the
magnitude of the base answer. If they concentrate on near-zero answers --- where the
sensitivity score is unstable by construction (\S\ref{sec:method}) --- the
``wandering'' reading would need qualification, since such items are also the ones
most likely to sit near a grading boundary. We flag this as untested rather than
assume it away.

\input{table8_wording_2x2.tex}

\paragraph{What the accuracy number hides here.}
A single accuracy pass over the base and paraphrased prompts yields
\pairBaseSevenB\% and \pairVarSevenB\% (Table~\ref{tab:wording-2x2}), a difference
that a careless comparison could report as an effect of prompt wording. Re-scoring
the same generations against the reference shows the difference rests on
\nbWordingFlips{} discordant items out of \nbWordingCovered{}, is not significant,
and reverses sign across models (\pairDeltaPPThreeB{} / \pairDeltaPPSevenB{} /
\pairDeltaPPFourteenB{} / \pairDeltaPPLlamaEightB{} points). Two distinct
comparisons are available --- \emph{answer agreement} with the base run, and
\emph{correctness} against the reference --- and they disagree by an order of
magnitude. Reporting only the first is not a conservative error: it manufactures
an effect.

\subsection{Where is the computation, and is the output on the same pathway?}
\label{sec:causal}
Table~\ref{tab:causal} locates the computation in depth.

\input{table3_causal.tex}

\paragraph{Scope.}
Of \nbCausalAttempts{} attempted problems, only \nbCausalValid{} satisfy the
strict inclusion rule (\nbCausalCleanWrong{} skipped as clean-wrong,
\nbCausalFlat{} as corruption-flat). This subset is strongly selected: it consists
of problems the model \emph{already solves}. Every statement in this subsection is
therefore scoped to solvable problems, not to the benchmark as a whole.

\paragraph{A band, not a peak --- and that is the result.}
Recovery rises across the early-middle layers and collapses after layer 18
(Figure~\ref{fig:causal}). The point estimate is maximal at layer \peakLayer{}
($+\peakRecovery$), with a bootstrap 95\% CI on that mean of
$[+\peakCiLo, +\peakCiHi]$. A paired bootstrap over problems, however, cannot
separate it from \nbNonseparableLayers{} other layers.

We regard this as an identifiability result rather than a failed localisation. At
\nbCausalValid{} exploitable problems, single-layer attribution is not supportable
in this setting: the data licence a \emph{range} claim and nothing finer. Studies
that report a specific critical layer from comparable sample sizes are, on our
estimate, reporting a point estimate whose uncertainty is larger than the
differences between adjacent layers --- which is a statement about what the
protocol can establish, not about the model alone.

\paragraph{How the band is defined.}
Rather than reading a range off the curve by eye, we define it formally: the plateau
is the maximal \emph{contiguous} run of layers whose recovery is not separable from
the peak under the paired bootstrap. This yields layers \plateauLo--\plateauHi{}
(\nbPlateauLayers{} sampled layers); the next sampled layer, 16, \emph{is} separable
from the peak, which is why the band stops there. Because layer sampling is
quantised (every \nbLayerStep{} layers), the boundaries are accurate to one sampling
step. We therefore claim a recoverable \emph{band} of layers \plateauLo--\plateauHi{}
rather than a single peak layer; only layers beyond $\sim$18, and the embedding
region (whose variance is an order of magnitude larger), are clearly
distinguishable.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=.72\linewidth]{causal_tracing_Qwen2.5-7B.png}
  \caption{Recovery rate as a function of the restored layer, with $\pm1$ standard
           deviation across problems. The dotted line at zero is the corrupted
           (no-restore) baseline, so the height above it is what restoration buys.
           Behaviour is recoverable over an early-middle band.}
  \label{fig:causal}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Relation to factual-recall localisation.}
In factual-recall tracing the recoverable signal typically occupies the middle of
the network (roughly the central third of depth). Our plateau (layers
\plateauLo--\plateauHi{} of \nbLayers, i.e.\ the first half) sits \emph{earlier}
than that. Two differences plausibly explain the discrepancy.
First, our corruption replaces \emph{numerical quantities} with a placeholder,
whereas factual-recall protocols replace a \emph{named subject}; collapsing the
numeric input removes information that has to be re-encoded early, so restoration
must intervene early. Second, the target here is a derived scalar rather than a
retrieved attribute, so the computation is spread over more layers. These are
hypotheses consistent with our data, not findings we can establish without
additional corruption types.

\paragraph{Separating execution from semantics.}
Restoring a clean state is only defined for problems whose clean run is correct,
so the recovery curve above cannot tell us whether restoration could \emph{rescue}
failures. We therefore instrument all \nbAllAttempts{} attempted problems and
record two outcomes at every restore point: whether the model emits a parseable
number (output execution) and whether that number is correct (semantics).
Table~\ref{tab:layer-parse-acc} shows the result, split by clean-run correctness.

\input{table6_layer_parse_vs_acc.tex}

Parseability is essentially layer-invariant: \pctParseCorrupt\% even for the
corrupted baseline, and \pctParseMin--\pctParseMax\% at every restore point. The
model always emits a number; corruption never makes it decline. Correctness, by
contrast, depends on the restored depth. For the \nbCleanCorrect{} problems the
clean run solves, restoration recovers \nbCleanCorrectK{} of them
(\pctAccCleanCorrectBest\%, Wilson 95\% CI $[\wilsonCcLo, \wilsonCcHi]$) at the best
layer --- suggestive but imprecise at this sample size. For the \nbCleanWrong{}
problems the best layer reaches only \nbCleanWrongK{} of \nbCleanWrong{}
(\pctAccCleanWrongBest\%, Wilson 95\% CI $[\wilsonCwLo, \wilsonCwHi]$), an interval
whose upper bound is an order of magnitude below the correct-baseline recovery and
which includes zero at several layers. The two pathways are therefore distinct, and
the failures are capability limits rather than misplaced representations that a
patch could repair.

\paragraph{What the accuracy number hides here.}
The accuracy metric collapses ``emitted a number'' and ``emitted the right number''
into one bit, and in this setting the first is nearly always true while the second
is mostly false. Measured separately, they behave differently under intervention:
format compliance is already at ceiling before any restoration and stays there,
whereas correctness moves with depth. A single number cannot express that
dissociation --- and an evaluation that silently discards unparseable outputs would
understate how rarely the parseability channel actually fails.

\subsection{Knowledge or execution? A format control}
\label{sec:cross}
Table~\ref{tab:cross-task} holds the model and the subject matter fixed and varies
only the answer format.

\input{table5_cross_task.tex}

The same Qwen2.5-7B gets \accMMLU\% of \nbMMLU{} multiple-choice physics items
right but only \accCotSevenB\% when it must produce the number itself. Since the
multiple-choice items test the same physics, missing knowledge cannot be the
explanation: the knowledge is retrievable, the numerical execution is not.

OlympiadBench is used \emph{only} to check the direction of the scale trend:
accuracy rises from \accOlySevenB\% to \accOlyFourteenB\% (+\gapOlyFourteenVsSeven{}
points), consistent with SciBench. Because its \pctOlympiadScorable\% gradable
coverage is biased towards items with clean scalar answers, we make no
absolute-level claim from this benchmark and never compare its values numerically
against SciBench; per-sub-domain counts are so small (six gradable optics items,
all incorrect) that we do not interpret them.

\subsection{Validity bound I: which stage fails, and can anyone tell?}
\label{sec:errors}
We annotated every SciBench chain-of-thought error with a three-way scheme --- E1
wrong physical law, E2 algebraic/arithmetic/unit error, E3 violated constraint ---
and repeated the annotation with a second, cross-family judge on the same samples.

\input{table4_errors.tex}

\paragraph{The finding is about observability, not about our judges.}
The reason two competent judges disagree here is mechanical: applying the wrong law
usually produces wrong arithmetic as well, so the \emph{observable text} is
compatible with either label. E1 and E2 are not separated by anything a judge can
see in the generation. The per-class pattern confirms it --- the judges agree
\pctAgreeEtwo\% of the time on E2 but only \pctAgreeEone\% on E1, i.e.\ agreement is
high exactly where the category is defined by surface evidence (an arithmetic slip
inside a correct derivation) and collapses where it would require inferring an
unobservable intention. This is a limitation of the taxonomy as a measurement
instrument, not an indictment of either model: no annotator, human or LLM, can
recover a distinction the text does not carry.

What survives the disagreement is therefore what both judges can see: E2 is the
modal category and E3 is rare under \emph{both} labellings, and the 7B/14B
distributions do not differ under either (same-family
$\chi^2=\chiSameFamily$, $p=\pChiSameFamily$; cross-family
$\chi^2=\chiCrossFamily$, $p=\pChiCrossFamily$). Overall agreement is
$\kappa=\kappaAnnotators$ (\pctAgreeAnnotators\% raw,
$\kappa_w=\kappaLinearWeighted$ linearly weighted). The larger model gets more
problems right rather than failing differently, which matches the format control:
the bottleneck is execution, not physical knowledge.

\paragraph{What the accuracy number hides here.}
``Error type'' is often reported as a single distribution from a single judge. With
two judges the distribution shifts by more than 20 points on the E1/E2 split, which
means the reported shares are partly a property of the annotator. The 7B/14B
\emph{comparison}, by contrast, is stable across judges --- so the same table
supports one claim robustly and another not at all, and only the agreement
measurement reveals which is which.

\subsection{Can prompt scaffolding repair the instability?}
\label{sec:scaffold}
If the paraphrase instability were caused by a missing solution scaffold, telling
the model to write down the knowns and unknowns explicitly should help. At a
matched token budget on the same \nbScafCot{} problems it changes accuracy from
\accScafCot\% to \accScafStruct\% (\scafDeltaPP{} points, McNemar $p=\scafP$)
--- no reliable gain (Table~\ref{tab:scaffold}). The failure is a capability
property, not a missing scaffold.

\subsection{Validity bound II: an output-format ceiling}
\label{sec:format}
The second bound is on the evaluation apparatus, and it is large enough to change
conclusions. Between \pctNoAnswerCot\% and \pctNoAnswerStruct\% of outputs never emit
the requested \texttt{ANSWER:} line. Output lengths stay far below the token budget,
so this is a format-compliance failure, not truncation --- the models simply do not
follow the formatting instruction.

Two grading policies are then available for the same generations: strict (count
format violations as failures) and fallback (recover a number from the last line).
The lower block of Table~\ref{tab:scaffold} pools both prompts and compares outputs
with and without the marker: accuracy is similar in the two groups, so the fallback
does not visibly inflate the headline numbers. That is a relief for our own results
but not for the field: on identical generations the two policies give different
ceilings, and which one a paper used is usually not reported. A strict-format
evaluation of the same models would therefore publish a lower number without
disclosing that the difference is compliance, not capability --- the same
failure mode as the annotation bound above, in a second place.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\paragraph{Statistics of the causal probe.}
The causal analysis rests on \nbCausalValid{} strictly-included problems, and the
layer-parse analysis on \nbCleanCorrect{} correct-baseline problems. Both are
small: we report bootstrap intervals for the recovery curve and Wilson intervals
for the small proportions, we do not claim a single peak layer, and we scope the
conclusions to problems the model can already solve.
\paragraph{Annotator dependence.}
Error-type shares are annotator-dependent ($\kappa=\kappaAnnotators$,
$\kappa_w=\kappaLinearWeighted$) and are reported coarsely for that reason. The
cross-family judge is still an LLM, so this is a check against family-specific
bias, not a substitute for human annotation.
\paragraph{Benchmark coverage.}
OlympiadBench numbers refer to the \pctOlympiadScorable\% of problems with
numerically gradable references and are used only for the direction of the scale
trend.
\paragraph{Backend and hardware.}
All runs use a single A800-80G with the \texttt{transformers} backend and no
continuous batching, so wall-clock times are not comparable to a vLLM deployment;
greedy decoding makes accuracies backend-independent.
\paragraph{Probe construction.}
The paraphrase probe uses deterministic rule-based rewriting; a model-generated
paraphrase would be stronger but introduces rewrite quality as a confound. The
scaffolding probe used one prompt design at one budget, so its null result is
evidence against that intervention, not proof of impossibility. Finally, all models
are evaluated zero-shot with a single fixed prompt per condition, and no per-model
prompt tuning is performed, because prompt sensitivity is itself one of the
phenomena measured. Note that the two conditions of the scaffolding comparison are
\emph{each} a single fixed prompt (standard chain-of-thought and the structured
variant) evaluated at a matched token budget, so the comparison isolates the
prompt content rather than the budget.
